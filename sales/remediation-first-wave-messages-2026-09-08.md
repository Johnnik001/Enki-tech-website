If yes, I can share the checklist; if not, no need to force a fit.

# Follow-up 1 — partner
> Quick follow-up. The model is simply overflow implementation capacity after your assessment / advisory work, with a bounded scope and handback evidence. If that is occasionally a delivery bottleneck, I can send the checklist and scope model.

# Reply qualification questions

If the reply is positive, ask only these three first:
1. What is the source of the backlog — internal review, audit, security tool, customer assessment or regulatory work?
2. Which Microsoft services are in scope?
3. What currently prevents the findings from being closed: ownership, expertise, change windows, dependencies or available delivery capacity?

Only after those answers discuss scope, timing and commercial format.

# First-wave success criteria

Strong signal:
- 2+ meaningful replies from 8 first touches; or
- 1 discovery call with a confirmed implementation backlog; or
- 1 partner willing to test Enki on a real client work package.

Weak signal:
- polite interest in the checklist but no backlog / no implementation need.

Negative signal:
- buyers consistently say implementation capacity is not a problem, or the issue sits entirely outside the Microsoft scope.

If the signal is weak or negative, change the message before changing the product.
