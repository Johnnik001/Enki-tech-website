## Recommended post

**The CRA reporting clock starts on 11 September. Is the operational workflow actually ready?**

From 11 September 2026, the Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligations under Article 14 apply to manufacturers of products with digital elements.

ENISA’s Single Reporting Platform is scheduled to become operational on the same date for mandatory reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents.

The deadlines are tight:

→ early warning within 24 hours of awareness
→ notification within 72 hours
→ final reporting afterwards, depending on the event type

But the difficult part is not knowing that a deadline exists.

The difficult part is making the operating chain work when the clock starts:

**Awareness → product/version mapping → ownership → evidence retrieval → reporting data → remediation → verification**

A useful readiness test should answer practical questions:

- Who records the authoritative awareness time?
- Who owns the 24h/72h workflow and who is the backup?
- Can the required technical evidence be retrieved quickly?
- Is the Assigned Representative access path to the SRP ready, including EU Login and MFA?
- Can corrective action be linked back to evidence of closure?

Enki Tech has built a technical dry-run model to test this workflow before a real event puts the organisation under deadline pressure.

> The key issue for me is the gap between knowing the reporting rule and being able to execute the technical workflow under time pressure. If ownership, evidence retrieval or remediation handoffs depend on one person, the process is fragile before the first real incident even starts the clock.
